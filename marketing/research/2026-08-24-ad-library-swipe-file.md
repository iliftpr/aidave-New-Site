# Proven Ads Swipe File — AI Receptionist / Missed-Call Text-Back Space
**Source:** Meta Ad Library (public, primary-source only — no Foreplay or paid swipe tool). Pulled 2026-08-24 via `ads_library_search` (US, ACTIVE) across 10 search terms, cross-checked with Firecrawl scrapes of individual ad snapshot pages.

**Honesty note up front:** This niche is in an active land-grab. Of ~40 in-niche ads reviewed, only **two advertisers cleared 30+ days running** (Loman, Nextiva) — everything else launched in the **last 0–5 days**, heavy fresh-campaign churn, not a deep bench of multi-month winners. Reporting that as a finding, not padding the "proven" bar. Two accounts (Polsia, Velinor AI Solutions) had swapped their creative entirely between my first search and my scrape hours later — same Page, unrelated product (fragrance, B2B outbound leads). A real signal about this ecosystem (rented/flipped ad accounts), noted where relevant below.

## (a) Top 5 templates to mimic first

Ranked by proof strength (longevity + variant count) first, mechanism quality second — not padded to 5-per-vertical where the pool was thin (contractors and dental both had real 30-day-plus tenure gaps, noted above rather than hidden):

1. **Loman, "Silence the ringing phone"** (restaurant, 43 days, 2 variants) — the strongest proof point in the file. Study the exact-number scene-mirror structure directly.
2. **ReceptionAide** (restaurant, video) — steal the "Call now" CTA mechanic; it's the single best fit for a text-back-first product.
3. **Imprynt** (contractor, video) — closest existing template to iLift's own text-back-first, hyperlocal ("Built for Miami" → "Built for Long Island") positioning.
4. **BiteBot.io** (dental, 30+ live variants) — not for the copy (too bundled/kitchen-sink) but for the free-tool-as-lead-magnet mechanic and the sheer scale of concurrent testing as validation that this creative direction gets budget.
5. **Extendly** (HVAC, image) — cleanest "name the ICP, stake the emergency hour, state the mechanism" skeleton to adapt per trade.

## (b) Five most-overused patterns (avoid these)

1. **"Never miss a call [again]"** — appeared as the literal headline or a paraphrase in the majority of ads reviewed (Task Urge, Polsia, Imprynt's own CTA link, Vantage AI). Explicitly on iLift's own banned list — for good reason, it's the single most saturated phrase in the category.
2. **Personifying the AI with a human name/pronoun** ("Connie," "Loman," "Xbert," "Aiva") — common enough to read as a category convention now, which cuts against standing out, and edges toward the "AI voice" framing iLift wants to avoid.
3. **Unsourced big-dollar loss claims** ("$100,000 gone," "$50k/MO") — punchy but unfalsifiable; several ads use a large round number with no math shown.
4. **"24/7" as the entire value prop** — present in nearly every ad's headline; on its own it's table stakes, not a differentiator.
5. **Generic "Learn More" CTA on a low-specificity headline** (Nextiva, Vantage AI, Velinor's now-swapped creative) — the ads with the weakest apparent commitment (shortest runs, thinnest copy) cluster here; specificity and an unusual CTA (like ReceptionAide's "Call now") correlate with the longer-running, better-crafted ads.

## (c) What the Ad Library can and cannot tell us

The Ad Library gives real creative text, format, advertiser identity, and a `Started running on` date with a rough active-time counter — a genuine, verifiable longevity proxy, and in this pull it separated two 40+ day survivors (Loman, Nextiva) and one high-variant tester (BiteBot.io) from a much larger pile of ads under a week old. It does **not** provide spend, impressions (beyond a coarse "low impression count" flag), click-through/conversion data, or targeting — so "long-running" proxies "the advertiser believes it's working," not proof of results. It's also not a fixed archive: two Pages here (Polsia, Velinor AI Solutions) had already swapped live creative to an unrelated product between the initial search and the follow-up scrape hours later — treat point-in-time snapshots as a moving target.
